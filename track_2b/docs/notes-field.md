# Submission form: notes field (≤ 5000 characters; jurors read it)

Gleichlaut keeps the French and Italian of Switzerland's drug-reimbursement conditions in step with the German. The Federal Office of Public Health (BAG) publishes 1,688 such conditions ("limitations") in its list of reimbursed medicines, the Spezialitätenliste (SL), in three languages; each language version is binding. Review register: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/ · demo video: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/demo.mp4

Why it matters: in today's list, Nemluvio is reimbursed every 8 weeks in German and French and "ogni 4 settimane" in Italian; Diaphin's surcharge is 6% in German and French and 12% in Italian. For one Nemluvio patient the two readings differ by CHF 13,577 a year in reimbursed doses; one Amvuttra patient's stop decision is worth CHF 338,743 a year at list price. A condition that says something different in each language cannot be applied consistently.

1. The audit finds these today, with no model. It reads every limitation in all three languages and turns the register into 18 issues to decide, each with the three texts side by side. Two blind Claude annotators confirm 12 findings that change a condition, 10 of them re-confirming the census list its rules were built around: Nemluvio, Diaphin, Cresemba's 16 kg limit missing in French, a French-only 62.5 kg rule in three etanercept products, Dupixent's weight tiers. Its experimental checks (off by default) found what nobody had listed: Amvuttra's French stop rule says an NIS increase "≤ 10" points where German and Italian say "> 10"; the Italian of Beyonttra and Vyndaqel says "on deve" (a typo for "non deve"), so a ban on combining reads as an obligation. A German phase-1 pilot pack (decision memo, project mandate, protection-needs analysis, one case sheet per issue for the records system) is in track_2b/docs/pilot/.

2. Drafting with Apertus. When the German changes, Apertus drafts the French and Italian: the old text stays locked outside the edit, numbers never reach the model (code writes them back), and frozen deterministic checks, not a model, decide which drafts are ready and say why in the reviewer's language. The default is Apertus 8B on an ordinary CPU, fully offline. On 166 held-out amendments its drafts need 33.1% fewer word-level edits to reach BAG's published text, and the drafts marked ready carry no meaning error in the blind labels (the annotators caught every planted error and flagged 5 of 17 real errors BAG had corrected). Apertus 70B saves 42.4% on the shared Public AI endpoint (which model it serves we could not verify), at CHF 0.0024 per draft at a Swiss provider's price; an office admits its own endpoint through a certificate.

Where it plugs in: BAG's ePL editor keeps one field per language (drafts fill French and Italian when the German changes); the applicant is asked in the triennial review whether the translations are congruent (the audit gives BAG its own check, for the whole list); translators get XLIFF for STAR Transit and a TBX termbase mined from the register; decisions export as CSV for the federal records system (GEVER).

Scope and next steps: the audit ships first and re-checks the whole list every month in seconds. Drafting already runs in shadow mode on the live public feed; on the August and September 2026 lists most drafts went to review, so it is a review aid today; two pre-registered prompt variants lowered the flag rate but did not pass our rule, and the shipped default stays. The audit's findings are frozen and hashed as predictions, to be scored against BAG's own next corrections.

Check us: `make run` in track_2b/ (Docker, CPU, about two minutes, no network) re-runs every recorded Apertus request and re-derives every number; claims.tsv maps every number in the README, the report and these notes to a results file. eval/prereg/PREREG.md holds every pre-registered rule with an index, including the runs that failed; eval/labels/ holds every label, worklist and annotator instruction, so anyone, including a BAG translator, can re-run the labelling.
