# Submission form: notes field (≤ 5000 characters; jurors read it)

Gleichlaut keeps the French and Italian of Switzerland's drug-reimbursement conditions (the 1,688 limitations of BAG's Spezialitätenliste) in step with the German. When BAG amends a condition in German, Apertus drafts the matching French and Italian change, and an audit lists every condition whose languages already disagree. Numbers never reach the model: they become slot codes that code writes back. Try the review register: https://mi11ione.github.io/gleichlaut/

Why numbers: in today's register, 10 limitations carry 5 divergences between languages, and every one is a number (Nemluvio every 8 weeks in German, "ogni 4 settimane" in Italian; Diaphin 6% vs 12%; a French-only 62.5 kg condition in 3 etanercept products; Cresemba; Dupixent).

Results, pre-registered on 166 held-out amendments (SL changes Jun 2025 to Feb 2026) with the checks hashed before any output existed: Apertus 70B drafts equal BAG's published text 17.5% of the time vs 6.0% for the no-model start (+11.4, 95% CI [4.8, 18.7]) and +6.6 [1.8, 12.0] over rules+, our strongest no-model baseline; they cut the edits still needed by 42.4% [31.0, 54.1]; 39.8% come out ready, with 0 meaning errors, stale changes or German left among them; a rerun with a new seed passes the same rule. The audit reduces 1,688 limitations to 27 findings: all 10 known divergent limitations plus 6 new confirmed problems, among them Keytruda's Italian field holding the French text since 1 Aug 2026. CHF 0.0024 per draft at a Swiss provider's price.

Model size measured both ways: on SL conditions the 8B flags 85.0% of drafts, so SL runs the 70B on a certified endpoint the user controls; on federal statutes the 70B is no better than the 8B (exact 48.3% vs 50.0%), so statutes run the 8B on a CPU. No model judges anything: deterministic checks decide, and explain each flag in the reviewer's language.

Where it plugs in: BAG's ePL editor keeps one field per language (Draft fills FR/IT when DE changes); applicants declare in BAG's triennial review that translations are congruent (Audit gives BAG its own check of that, for the whole register); translators get XLIFF 1.2/2.1 for STAR Transit; drafts come out as FHIR in the feed's own shape. Shadow mode (`gleichlaut month`) runs on the public feed each month and grades itself against BAG's publication.

Check us: `make run` in track_2b/ (Docker, CPU, about 30 s, no network) re-executes the recorded Apertus runs from the recorded responses (all 285 requests rebuilt byte-for-byte), re-scores every number, and validates XLIFF/FHIR. claims.tsv maps every number in the README, report and these notes to a results file; eval/prereg/PREREG.md holds every rule, written before its run (our first out-of-sample run failed its rule; it is reported); eval/labels/ holds blind, triple-annotated labels anyone can re-run; docs/failure-modes.md lists where Apertus fell short and what does that job instead.
