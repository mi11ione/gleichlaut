# Submission form: notes field (≤ 5000 characters; jurors read it)

Gleichlaut keeps the French and Italian of Switzerland's drug-reimbursement conditions (the 1,688 limitations of BAG's Spezialitätenliste) in step with the German. When BAG amends a condition in German, Apertus drafts the matching French and Italian change, and an audit lists every condition whose languages already disagree. Numbers never reach the model: they become slot codes that code writes back. Try the review register: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/ (demo video: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/demo.mp4)

Why numbers: in today's register, 10 limitations carry 5 divergences between languages that change a condition, and every one of them is a number (Nemluvio every 8 weeks in German, "ogni 4 settimane" in Italian; Diaphin 6% vs 12%; a French-only 62.5 kg condition in 3 etanercept products; Cresemba; Dupixent).

The audit runs today, with no model. It reduces 1,688 limitations to 27 findings in 18 issues to decide; blind annotators confirm 16 (59.3%): all 10 known divergent limitations (a check, not a recall measure: the rules were written around the census that found them), five new typos next to numbers, and Keytruda's Italian field holding the French text since 1 Aug 2026. Learning from BAG's own corrections, it finds four French texts that still name the wrong drug class. On MiGeL and the Analysenliste, which it was never designed on, 7 of 11 findings are real. On 1,000 divergences planted into clean limitations its recall is 77.2%: 93.0% for a changed number, 73.0% for a changed unit, 75.0% for a deleted clause; 55.5% at any number; operators and negations are the next rules.

Drafting, pre-registered on 166 held-out amendments (SL changes Jun 2025 to Feb 2026) with the checks hashed before any output existed. The default, Apertus 8B Q8_0 on a CPU (16 GiB, air-gapped), drafts BAG's published text exactly 17.5% of the time vs 6.0% for the no-model start (+11.4, 95% CI [4.8, 18.7]), cuts the edits still needed by 33.1% [20.8, 43.9], and has 0 errors among its 62 ready drafts. The 70B, where an endpoint you control is available, has the same identical-draft rate, cuts edits by 42.4% [31.0, 54.1] (paired difference 9.3 points [-1.3, 20.2]), adds +6.6 [1.8, 12.0] over rules+, our strongest no-model baseline, and its 39.8% ready drafts hold 0 meaning errors, stale changes or German left; a rerun with a new seed passes the same rule; CHF 0.0024 per draft at a Swiss provider's price. On two live months (Aug to Sep, Sep to Oct 2026, run air-gapped on CPU runners) no draft marked ready changed a condition's meaning, but neither month met our pre-registered rule: the checks flagged 81.8–92.7% of drafts, so on live data the drafting is a review aid and the audit is what an office runs first. On federal statutes the 70B is no better than the 8B (exact 48.3% vs 50.0%). No model judges anything: deterministic checks decide, and explain each flag in the reviewer's language.

Where it plugs in: BAG's ePL editor keeps one field per language (Draft fills FR/IT when DE changes); applicants declare in BAG's triennial review that translations are congruent (Audit gives BAG its own check of that, for the whole register); translators get XLIFF 1.2/2.1 for STAR Transit; drafts come out as FHIR in the feed's own shape. Shadow mode (`gleichlaut month`) runs on the public feed each month and grades itself against BAG's publication. A house termbase mined from the register (TBX for TermStar) names the term in 22 of the 25 ready 8B Q8_0 drafts that still need a fix.

Check us: `make run` in track_2b/ (Docker, CPU, about a minute, no network; `make local-run` without Docker) re-executes the recorded Apertus runs from the recorded responses (all 285 requests rebuilt byte-for-byte), re-scores every number, and validates XLIFF/FHIR. claims.tsv maps every number in the README, report and these notes to a results file; eval/prereg/PREREG.md holds every rule, written before its run (our first out-of-sample run failed its rule; it is reported); eval/labels/ holds the blind Claude annotators' labels (two plus a tie-breaker), their worklists and instructions; docs/failure-modes.md lists where Apertus fell short and what does that job instead.
