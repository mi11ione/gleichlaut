# Submission form: notes field (≤ 5000 characters; jurors read it)

Gleichlaut keeps the French and Italian of Switzerland's drug-reimbursement conditions (the 1,688 limitations of BAG's Spezialitätenliste) in step with the German. When BAG amends a condition in German, Apertus drafts the matching French and Italian change, and an audit lists every condition whose languages already disagree. Numbers never reach the model: they become slot codes that code writes back. Try the review register: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/ (demo video: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/demo.mp4)

Why numbers: in today's register, 10 limitations carry 5 divergences between languages that change a condition, and every one of them is a number (Nemluvio every 8 weeks in German, "ogni 4 settimane" in Italian; Diaphin 6% vs 12%; a French-only 62.5 kg condition in 3 etanercept products; Cresemba; Dupixent).

Results, pre-registered on 166 held-out amendments (SL changes Jun 2025 to Feb 2026) with the checks hashed before any output existed: Apertus 70B drafts equal BAG's published text 17.5% of the time vs 6.0% for the no-model start (+11.4, 95% CI [4.8, 18.7]) and +6.6 [1.8, 12.0] over rules+, our strongest no-model baseline; they cut the edits still needed by 42.4% [31.0, 54.1]; 39.8% come out ready, with 0 meaning errors, stale changes or German left among them; a rerun with a new seed passes the same rule. The audit reduces 1,688 limitations to 27 findings: all 10 known divergent limitations, five new typos next to numbers, and Keytruda's Italian field holding the French text since 1 Aug 2026. Learning from BAG's own corrections, it finds four French texts that still name the wrong drug class. On MiGeL and the Analysenliste, which it was never designed on, 7 of 11 findings are real. On 1,000 divergences planted into clean limitations its recall is 77.2% (93.0% for a changed number bound to a unit, its scope; 55.5% at any number; operators and negations are the next rules). CHF 0.0024 per draft at a Swiss provider's price.

Model size measured both ways, pre-registered: on SL conditions both shipped 8B builds pass the same rule on a CPU, so the default (the 8B Q8_0 build, 16 GiB RAM) runs fully air-gapped. Q8_0 ties the 70B on identical drafts (17.5%) with 0 errors among its 62 ready drafts; the 70B saves more edits (42.4% vs 33.1%, paired difference 9.3 points [-1.3, 20.2]; vs 20.9% for Q4_K_M) and its ready drafts rarely need a fix (3 of 66). On federal statutes the 70B is no better than the 8B (exact 48.3% vs 50.0%). No model judges anything: deterministic checks decide, and explain each flag in the reviewer's language.

Where it plugs in: BAG's ePL editor keeps one field per language (Draft fills FR/IT when DE changes); applicants declare in BAG's triennial review that translations are congruent (Audit gives BAG its own check of that, for the whole register); translators get XLIFF 1.2/2.1 for STAR Transit; drafts come out as FHIR in the feed's own shape. Shadow mode (`gleichlaut month`) runs on the public feed each month and grades itself against BAG's publication; the first month (1 Sep to 1 Oct 2026) ran air-gapped on CPU runners: of the 14 drafts marked ready across the three models, none has an error. A house termbase mined from the register (TBX for TermStar) names the term in 22 of the 25 ready 8B Q8_0 drafts that still need a fix.

Check us: `make run` in track_2b/ (Docker, CPU, about a minute, no network; `make local-run` without Docker) re-executes the recorded Apertus runs from the recorded responses (all 285 requests rebuilt byte-for-byte), re-scores every number, and validates XLIFF/FHIR. claims.tsv maps every number in the README, report and these notes to a results file; eval/prereg/PREREG.md holds every rule, written before its run (our first out-of-sample run failed its rule; it is reported); eval/labels/ holds the blind Claude annotators' labels (two plus a tie-breaker), their worklists and instructions; docs/failure-modes.md lists where Apertus fell short and what does that job instead.
