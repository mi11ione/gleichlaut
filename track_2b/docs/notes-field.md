# Submission form: notes field (≤ 5000 characters; jurors read it)

Gleichlaut keeps the French and Italian of Switzerland's drug-reimbursement conditions (the 1,688 limitations of BAG's Spezialitätenliste) in step with the German. When BAG amends a condition in German, Apertus drafts the matching French and Italian change, and an audit lists every condition whose languages already disagree. Numbers never reach the model: they become slot codes that code writes back. Try the review register: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/ (demo video: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/demo.mp4)

Why numbers: in today's register, 10 limitations carry 5 divergences between languages that change a condition, and every one of them is a number (Nemluvio every 8 weeks in German, "ogni 4 settimane" in Italian; Diaphin 6% vs 12%; a French-only 62.5 kg condition in 3 etanercept products; Cresemba; Dupixent).

The audit runs today, with no model, and finds what nobody had listed. In the 6 Oct 2026 register: Amvuttra's French stop rule says an NIS increase "≤ 10" points where German and Italian say "> 10"; the Italian of Beyonttra and Vyndaqel says "on deve" (for "non deve"), so a ban on combining reads as an obligation; Keytruda's Italian field has held the French text since 1 Aug 2026; and four French texts still name the wrong drug class. Its main check reduces 1,688 limitations to 27 findings, which become 18 issues, one decision each; two blind annotators (a third on disagreements) confirm 17 as real (63.0%), including all 10 known divergent limitations (the list its rules were built around, so not a recall measure). On 1,000 divergences planted into clean limitations it finds 93.0% of changed numbers, 73.0% of changed units and 75.0% of deleted clauses. The Amvuttra and Beyonttra finds come from pre-registered checks for operators and negations that ship off by default, because only 6 of their 31 findings are confirmed. On MiGeL and the Analysenliste, which it was never designed on, 6 of 11 findings are real.

Drafting, pre-registered on 166 held-out amendments (SL changes Jun 2025 to Feb 2026) with the checks hashed before any output existed. The verdict: the drafts cut the edits a translator still needs to reach BAG's published text by a third on the air-gapped default, Apertus 8B Q8_0 on a CPU (33.1% fewer, 95% CI [20.8, 43.9], about 670 word edits a month at today's volume), and by 42.4% with the 70B where an endpoint you control is available; no draft marked ready changes a meaning (0 among 62 ready 8B drafts, 95% upper bound 5.8%; 0 among 66 for the 70B, confirmed by a fully blind relabel). 17.5% of drafts are word for word what BAG published, against 6.0% for the no-model start; that is a harsh bar, since two correct translations rarely match. On two live months no draft marked ready changed a meaning either, but neither month met our pre-registered rule: the checks flagged 81.8–92.7% of drafts, so on live data the drafting is a review aid and the audit is what an office runs first. A 70B draft costs CHF 0.0024 at a Swiss provider's price. No model judges anything: deterministic checks decide, and explain each flag in the reviewer's language.

Where it plugs in: BAG's ePL editor keeps one field per language (Draft fills FR/IT when DE changes); applicants declare in BAG's triennial review that translations are congruent (Audit gives BAG its own check of that, for the whole register); translators get XLIFF 1.2/2.1 for STAR Transit; drafts come out as FHIR in the feed's own shape. Shadow mode (`gleichlaut month`) runs on the public feed each month and grades itself against BAG's publication. A house termbase mined from the register (TBX for TermStar) names the term in 22 of the 25 ready 8B Q8_0 drafts that still need a fix.

Check us: `make run` in track_2b/ (Docker, CPU, about two minutes, no network; `make local-run` without Docker) re-executes the recorded Apertus runs from the recorded responses (all 285 requests rebuilt byte-for-byte), re-scores every number, and validates XLIFF/FHIR. claims.tsv maps every number in the README, report and these notes to a results file; eval/prereg/PREREG.md holds every rule with an index; all but R0 (which predates the repository) and R7 (written after its first outputs) were committed before their runs, and the default build was chosen after both 8B results; the failed runs are reported; eval/labels/ holds the blind Claude annotators' labels (two plus a tie-breaker), their worklists and instructions; docs/failure-modes.md lists where Apertus fell short and what does that job instead.
