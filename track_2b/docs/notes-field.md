Switzerland's drug-reimbursement conditions are law in three languages. Gleichlaut finds where they disagree, and keeps them in step with Apertus. Review register: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/ · 2-minute demo: https://gleichlaut.mi11ion.io/demo.mp4

The problem, in today's official list: Nemluvio is reimbursed every 8 weeks in German and French and "ogni 4 settimane" in Italian; at the published price the two readings differ by CHF 13,577 a year per patient. Amvuttra's French stop rule says an NIS increase "≤ 10" points where German and Italian say "> 10"; one patient's stop decision is worth CHF 338,743 a year at list price. Diaphin's surcharge is 6% in German and French and 12% in Italian. Same drug, same law, three answers. The Federal Office of Public Health (BAG) publishes 1,688 such conditions ("limitations") in its Spezialitätenliste (SL); each language version is binding.

1. The audit, today, with no model. Gleichlaut reads every limitation in German, French and Italian and turns the list into 18 issues to decide, each with the three texts side by side and the conflicting values marked. Two blind Claude annotators confirm 12 findings that change a reimbursement condition: Nemluvio, Diaphin, Cresemba's 16 kg limit missing in French, a French-only 62.5 kg rule in three etanercept products, Dupixent's weight tiers. Its operator and negation module found what nobody had listed: Amvuttra's reversed stop rule, and an Italian typo in Beyonttra and Vyndaqel ("on deve" for "non deve") that turns a ban on combining into an obligation. On 1,000 divergences planted into clean texts it finds 93.0% of changed numbers.

2. Drafting, with Apertus. When BAG changes the German, Apertus drafts the French and Italian. Numbers never reach the model: code writes them back. Frozen checks, not a model, decide which drafts are ready and explain each flag in the reviewer's own language. On 166 held-out amendments, with every check frozen before the test, Apertus 8B on an ordinary CPU, fully offline, needs 33.1% fewer edits to reach BAG's published text and its ready drafts carry 0 meaning errors; Apertus 70B saves 42.4% at CHF 0.0024 per draft. We measured where Apertus is strong and where code is stronger, and split the work accordingly; the 8B passes our pre-registered rule on a CPU, so it is the default and nothing leaves the building.

3. Built to be used. It runs on one CPU server or a laptop, Docker or plain Python, Apache-2.0 code and weights; at runtime it reads only BAG's own public feed. It fits the existing process: drafts map onto the French and Italian fields of BAG's ePL editor; translators get XLIFF for STAR Transit and a TBX termbase mined from BAG's own wording; decisions export as CSV for the federal records system (GEVER); the audit answers the "translations congruent?" question of BAG's triennial review for the whole list. A German phase-1 pack with a one-page decision memo and one case sheet per issue is ready to hand over (track_2b/docs/pilot/). The same audit runs on MiGeL and the Analysenliste, BAG's other trilingual lists. Shadow mode drafts each new SL release on the live public feed every month and grades itself against what BAG publishes, and the audit's findings are frozen as hashed predictions, scored against BAG's own next corrections.

Check us: `make run` in track_2b/ (Docker, CPU, a few minutes, no network) re-executes every recorded Apertus call and re-derives every number; claims.tsv maps each number in the README, the report and these notes to its results file. Every rule is pre-registered (eval/prereg/PREREG.md), every label made under a published blind protocol anyone can re-run (eval/labels/).
